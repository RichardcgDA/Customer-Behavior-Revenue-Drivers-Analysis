# Customer-Behavior-Revenue-Drivers-Analysis
**Tools:** Python | Pandas | NumPy | Matplotlib | Seaborn | SciPy
### Exploring Customer Behavior, Revenue Relationships, and Churn Factors at NovaRetail+

##  Project Overview

Which customer behavior factors are most strongly associated with the annual revenue generated by an e-commerce platform?

This project analyzes customer data from **NovaRetail+**, a Latin American e-commerce platform, to investigate relationships between customer behavior, purchasing activity, annual revenue, subscription status, and customer churn.

Using Python and statistical analysis, I explored a dataset containing **15,000 customer records and 12 variables**, applying correlation techniques to identify meaningful relationships and evaluate their potential business implications.

### Key Findings

- **0.967 Pearson correlation** between monthly purchases and annual revenue, indicating a very strong positive relationship.
- **0.579 Pearson correlation** between monthly visits and targeted advertising spend, indicating a moderate positive relationship.
- Customer churn showed no strong individual correlation with the demographic, behavioral, or categorical variables analyzed.
- Monthly purchases and annual revenue exhibited substantial redundancy, highlighting a potential multicollinearity issue in predictive modeling.

##  Business Objectives

- Identify customer behavior variables most strongly associated with annual revenue.
- Investigate relationships between purchasing activity, website visits, customer satisfaction, and advertising expenditure.
- Evaluate potential relationships between customer characteristics and churn.
- Compare numerical, binary, and categorical variables using appropriate statistical methods.
- Translate statistical findings into actionable business recommendations.

##  Dataset Overview

##  Methodology

### 1. Data Exploration & Preparation

- Inspected dataset dimensions, column types, and descriptive statistics.
- Evaluated numerical, binary, and categorical variables.
- Converted customer age into an appropriate integer data type.
- Examined distributions, ranges, and potential anomalies.
- Documented analytical assumptions before performing statistical tests.

### 2. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

- Generated descriptive statistics for numerical variables.
- Examined customer age, income, purchases, visits, and satisfaction distributions.
- Analyzed premium membership and churn proportions.
- Evaluated device and geographic region distributions.
- Created correlation heatmaps and scatterplots to identify relevant relationships.

### 3. Statistical Correlation Analysis

Applied different statistical methods according to variable types:

- **Pearson Correlation:** Evaluated linear relationships between numerical variables.
- **Spearman Correlation:** Examined monotonic relationships between numerical variables.
- **Point-Biserial Correlation:** Investigated relationships between binary and continuous variables.
- **Cramér's V:** Measured associations between categorical variables.

Statistical significance was evaluated using p-values, while effect sizes were considered when interpreting practical business relevance.

##  Key Findings

### 1. Monthly Purchases & Annual Revenue

- Pearson correlation: **0.967**
- Spearman correlation: **0.967**

A very strong positive relationship was identified between monthly purchases and annual revenue.

Both variables appear to capture substantially overlapping information, raising the possibility that annual revenue is mathematically related to purchase frequency.

**Business implication:** Including both variables in the same predictive model could introduce severe multicollinearity and unstable coefficients. Further validation of their underlying relationship is recommended.

### 2. Monthly Visits & Targeted Advertising Spend

- Pearson correlation: **0.579**
- Spearman correlation: **0.559**

A moderate positive relationship was identified between monthly visits and targeted advertising expenditure.

Although the relationship is relatively consistent, considerable variability remains, suggesting that other factors may also influence advertising allocation and customer activity.

**Business implication:** Further investigation could help evaluate how customer engagement and advertising expenditure interact.

### 3. Customer Churn Analysis

Individual relationships between churn and the analyzed customer variables were generally weak.

Examples:

- Churn vs. annual revenue: r = -0.003
- Churn vs. satisfaction: r = -0.024
- Churn vs. monthly visits: r = -0.009
- Premium membership vs. annual revenue: r = 0.093

Cramér's V also showed weak associations between churn and categorical variables, with premium membership showing the strongest association at 0.1205.

**Business implication:** Retention strategies should not rely on individual customer characteristics alone. A multivariate approach may reveal combinations of variables that provide more useful churn insights.

##  Business Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following opportunities were identified:

**1. Improve customer revenue analysis**

Investigate the relationship between purchase frequency and annual revenue to better understand customer spending patterns and avoid redundant metrics in predictive models.

**2. Explore customer engagement patterns**

Further investigate the relationship between website visits and targeted advertising expenditure to improve customer engagement analysis.

**3. Develop more comprehensive churn analysis**

Evaluate combinations of demographic, behavioral, and subscription variables instead of relying on individual correlations to identify potential churn patterns.

**4. Explore predictive modeling opportunities**

Consider developing multivariate classification models, such as logistic regression or decision trees, to investigate customer churn using multiple variables simultaneously.

##  Limitations

- Correlation does not establish causation.
- The analysis primarily evaluates pairwise relationships rather than combined predictive effects.
- Large sample sizes can make very weak correlations statistically significant.
- Potential non-linear relationships and interactions may not be captured by the selected correlation methods.
- The suspected mathematical relationship between monthly purchases and annual revenue requires further validation.
